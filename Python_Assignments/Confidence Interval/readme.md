# Estimation and Confidence Intervals

## Background
In quality control processes, especially when dealing with high-value items, destructive sampling is often required to ensure product quality. Since testing destroys the item, only small sample sizes are feasible due to cost constraints. Statistical estimation techniques help draw reliable conclusions from limited data.

## Scenario
A manufacturer of computer print-heads aims to estimate the **average durability** of print-heads, measured by the number of characters printed before failure. Due to the destructive nature of testing, a small random sample of print-heads is tested until failure to assess durability.

## Data Description
A total of **15 print-heads** were randomly selected and tested.  
The durability of each print-head (in millions of characters printed before failure) is recorded as:

1.13, 1.55, 1.43, 0.92, 1.25, 1.36, 1.32, 0.85, 1.07, 1.48, 1.20, 1.33, 1.18, 1.22, 1.29


## Assignment Tasks

### a. 99% Confidence Interval Using Sample Standard Deviation
- Construct a **99% confidence interval** for the population mean durability.
- Assume the sample is representative of the population.
- Use the **t-distribution** since the population standard deviation is unknown.
- Explain the rationale behind using the t-distribution.

### b. 99% Confidence Interval Using Known Population Standard Deviation
- Assume the population standard deviation is **0.2 million characters**.
- Construct a **99% confidence interval** for the population mean.
- Use the **z-distribution** for interval estimation.
- Compare and interpret the difference between the two confidence intervals.

## Objective
The goal of this assignment is to understand statistical estimation techniques and the construction of confidence intervals under different assumptions about population variability.
